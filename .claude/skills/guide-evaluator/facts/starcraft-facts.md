## Races

- StarCraft II has **three playable races: Terran, Zerg, Protoss**. The campaigns split along
  them: Wings of Liberty (2010) Terran, Heart of the Swarm (2013) Zerg, Legacy of the Void (2015)
  Protoss. - source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarCraft_II
- The design is **asymmetric balance**: three races that play completely differently, balanced
  against each other rather than mirrored. This is the sharpest single contrast with the Age
  series, where civilizations share a common unit skeleton and differ by bonuses, unique units
  and tech-tree gaps.
  - source: WebSearch summary of https://simonhalliday.com/2019/09/04/starcraft-ii-a-study-in-asymmetrical-design/
    and https://gamecloud.net.au/features/opinion/what-makes-rts-games-fun-asymmetric-design
  ⚠️ CẦN NGƯỜI DUYỆT - the asymmetry claim is well established and uncontroversial, but it came
  from a search summary of opinion/analysis pieces rather than a primary design document.
- Illustrative asymmetry from the same summary: Zerg are built around swarm tactics where no
  single unit matches its counterpart - "four zerglings cost the same as a single protoss
  zealot". Each race has a distinct economic mechanic: Terran can mine extra minerals, Zerg
  generate larvae at the hatchery to spawn many units at once.
  ⚠️ CẦN NGƯỜI DUYỆT - unit costs and the exact wording of racial economy mechanics are
  patch-sensitive and came from a search summary. Use as illustration of the design idea, never
  as a stat claim.

## Ladder and ranking

- The ladder is **organized per geographic region** and consists of tiers called **leagues**:
  "ranging from the lowest bronze, through silver, gold, platinum, diamond, master and in turn
  the highest, grandmaster league." - source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarCraft_II
- That is seven leagues: **Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Diamond, Master, Grandmaster.**
  - source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarCraft_II
- ⚠️ CẦN NGƯỜI DUYỆT - the Grandmaster league's fixed size cap (commonly cited as 200 players per
  region) and the MMR mechanics behind promotion were NOT sourced this pass. The
  `ranked-aoe4-vs-ladder-starcraft` item needs the AoE4 ranked structure too, which is in neither
  this file nor `aoe4-facts.md`. Both halves are missing - that item is not writable yet.

## Macro, micro, APM

- **Micro** is fine-grained control - individual unit control in combat, and worker/gathering
  efficiency. **Macro** is the economic and strategic layer: keeping worker production going,
  managing resources, teching and age/tier progression. In AoE2 terms, "if it relates to
  resources or your base, it falls under macro."
  - source: WebSearch summary of https://steamcommunity.com/app/221380/discussions/0/350543389019096408/
    and https://www.e-sportsbetting.org/blog/macro-micro-and-apm-starcraft-terminology-101/
- **APM (actions per minute)** counts clicks and keyboard inputs per minute. A high APM means a
  player can physically manage more at once. StarCraft is widely described as demanding high APM
  to play at a high level.
  - source: WebSearch summary of https://www.e-sportsbetting.org/blog/macro-micro-and-apm-starcraft-terminology-101/
- One concrete mechanical difference found: **AoE2 offers unit formations selectable in a single
  click** (square, turtle and so on) whereas in StarCraft equivalent positioning is done manually
  through multiple clicks. This is a good, specific, non-judgmental example of why the two games
  feel different to the hands.
  - source: WebSearch summary of https://steamcommunity.com/app/105430/discussions/0/864960354170086771/
  ⚠️ CẦN NGƯỜI DUYỆT - from a Steam forum discussion, not documentation.
- ⚠️ The framing "AoE2 is macro-first, StarCraft is micro-first" appears in the same forum
  summary. It is a community opinion, NOT a sourced fact, and it edges toward implying one game
  is shallower. If an article uses it at all, attribute it as how players describe the feel, and
  keep both sides respectful.

## Supply, workers, base building

- StarCraft II uses **worker units gathering resources**, **supply mechanics capping unit
  production**, and base building - the standard RTS skeleton the Age series also uses.
  - source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarCraft_II
- ⚠️ CẦN NGƯỜI DUYỆT - the two-resource model (minerals and vespene gas) and the supply
  buildings per race (Supply Depot / Overlord / Pylon) are well known but were NOT captured from
  a source this pass. Do not state them until sourced; they are the single most likely thing an
  article will want and the single most embarrassing thing to get subtly wrong.

## Esports

- StarCraft II "was widely considered the largest esport in the world during its early years" and
  "has been credited as bringing esports to the rest of the world."
  - source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarCraft_II
- Major tournaments named: the **Global StarCraft II League (GSL)** and the **StarCraft II World
  Championship Series (WCS)**, the latter established in 2012.
  - source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarCraft_II
- ⚠️ CẦN NGƯỜI DUYỆT - the state of the SC2 competitive scene in 2026, including whether WCS
  still runs in that form and who currently funds the circuit, was NOT sourced. The
  `esports-aoe4-vs-starcraft2` item needs current-year facts on both sides and cannot be written
  from this file alone. Writing "SC2 esports đã tàn" from stale knowledge would be both wrong and
  disparaging.

## Access

- Since **2017**, StarCraft II's multiplayer, co-op mode and the first single-player campaign are
  **free-to-play**. This matters for any "dân StarCraft thử AoE4" article, because the barrier to
  trying the other game is asymmetric: SC2 is free, AoE4 is not.
  - source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/StarCraft_II
